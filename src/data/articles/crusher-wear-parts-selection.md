# Crusher Wear Parts Selection: A Practical Guide for Mining, Aggregate and Equipment Buyers

Crusher wear parts selection should begin with the machine reference and operating duty, not with a hardness number or a generic catalogue description. Identify the part—such as a jaw plate, cone mantle, concave or blow bar—then confirm the crusher model, part number, drawing or sample. Next, record the feed material, feed size, impact level, moisture, closed-side setting where relevant, current wear pattern and service record. These details help connect the part geometry and material to the way the component actually fails.

For a practical review, separate the decision into four questions: **Will the part fit? What wear mechanism is dominant? Which material family matches the duty? What evidence must be supplied with the order?** A jaw plate in a primary crushing application does not face the same conditions as a blow bar in an impact crusher. Likewise, two parts with similar names may require different profiles, dimensions, grades or inspection scopes.

The goal is not to choose the hardest available material. The goal is to choose a part that fits the machine, survives the actual combination of impact and abrasion, and can be checked against an agreed technical specification.

## Start With Machine Fit and Part Identity

Before comparing materials, establish exactly what is being replaced. A crusher wear part is a functional component with a defined position, profile and mounting interface. A similar-looking casting is not proof of compatibility.

### Information to identify the part

Provide as many of the following inputs as available:

- Crusher brand, model and series.
- Part number or the supplier’s reference number.
- Fixed or movable jaw position for jaw plates.
- Mantle, concave or bowl-liner position for cone equipment.
- Blow-bar position and rotor reference for impact equipment.
- Current drawing revision or 3D model.
- A worn sample or clear photographs of the working, mounting and side faces.
- Key dimensions, bolt-hole pattern, seating features and tooth or chamber profile.

If a complete drawing is not available, a part number, machine model, sample or clear worn-part photographs can still start a technical review. The missing dimensions and acceptance details should be confirmed before production. This approach is more reliable than selecting a replacement from a photograph alone.

## Jaw Plates: Match Profile, Position and Impact Duty

Jaw plates operate in a crushing chamber where the material is compressed and impacted between fixed and movable surfaces. Selection therefore depends on more than the phrase “jaw plate.” The fixed plate and movable plate have different positions and movement patterns, while chamber profile, feed size and closed-side setting influence how the working face wears.

High-manganese steel is listed on DINGDAR’s product data for jaw plates, including Mn13, Mn18 and variants. The site describes these grades as suitable for impact and abrasion duty, with alloy grades reviewed against the application. Manganese steel is designed to work-harden where impact is applied. That characteristic can be useful in primary and coarse crushing, but the actual result still depends on whether the operating duty provides sufficient impact and whether the casting fits the chamber correctly.

### Jaw plate selection checklist

For a jaw plate enquiry, record:

1. Crusher model and series.
2. Fixed or movable position.
3. Part number, drawing or sample reference.
4. Feed rock and typical feed size.
5. Closed-side setting if available.
6. Current service life and wear or failure pattern.
7. Photos of the working face, back and mounting face.
8. Quantity, delivery country and required inspection records.

The wear pattern is especially useful. Even thinning, localised wear, edge chipping, cracking or mounting damage can point to different technical questions. A supplier should review the geometry and operating conditions before confirming a grade or quoting a replacement.

## Cone Crusher Liners: Review the Chamber as a Pair

Cone crusher liners work as a matched chamber system. The mantle is the moving crushing member and the concave, or bowl liner, is the fixed member. Their geometry affects the crushing chamber, product shape and throughput, so a liner should not be selected as an isolated item without the corresponding machine and chamber information.

DINGDAR’s cone crusher product information covers mantle liners, concaves, bowl liners, gyratory concaves and upper housing castings. The listed material direction is high-manganese steel for mantles and concaves, with alloy grades reviewed where the chamber profile or application requires them. The same page identifies the importance of the model reference, drawing or sample, feed conditions and current wear pattern.

### Inputs for cone liner selection

A useful cone liner enquiry should include:

- Crusher make, model and chamber configuration.
- Mantle or concave reference and position.
- Drawing, part number or worn sample.
- Feed material, feed size and abrasiveness.
- Closed-side setting and relevant operating information.
- Current liner profile and wear distribution.
- Required machining, inspection and marking scope.

A liner that fits mechanically but does not match the required chamber profile can create avoidable problems. The review should therefore confirm the geometry, material family, inspection scope and quotation basis together. When the current liner is available, photographs showing the wear bands and remaining profile can help explain the duty, but they do not replace the part reference or drawing.

## Blow Bars and Impact Crusher Parts: Balance Abrasion and Toughness

Blow bars and impact plates experience repeated impact as material is accelerated through the rotor and impact zone. The material choice must reflect the balance between abrasive wear and impact severity. DINGDAR lists high-chromium, alloy and manganese grades for impact crusher wear parts, with the final choice reviewed against the model, drawing or sample and the operating conditions.

High-chromium cast iron is described on the site as a high-abrasion material family used for blow bars, impact plates and other high-wear applications. The materials page also explains its limits: high-chromium iron has lower toughness than manganese steel and may be unsuitable where heavy uncrushable impact or severe thermal cycling creates a cracking risk.

Manganese steel and alloy grades may be considered when the duty requires a different balance of toughness, strength or wear resistance. The correct answer depends on the failure mode observed in service. A bar that wears evenly may raise a different question from one that chips, cracks or breaks shortly after installation.

### Blow-bar information to provide

Include the impact crusher model, rotor or position reference, current material grade if known, feed material, feed size, moisture, tramp or uncrushable risk, and the observed failure pattern. Also specify whether the replacement must be supplied as-cast or machined, whether marking is required, and which inspection records must accompany the order.

Do not compare blow bars only by nominal hardness. Carbide structure, matrix support, section geometry and impact conditions all influence performance. A technical review should connect the material recommendation to the actual duty and should state what remains to be confirmed.

## Choose Material by Failure Mode, Not Hardness Alone

Material selection is a trade-off between wear resistance, toughness, strength, machinability and the way the part is loaded. The DINGDAR materials page identifies several families used across wear parts and cast components.

### High-manganese steel

Mn13, Mn18 and related grades are used for jaw plates, cone mantles and concaves, hammer heads and other impact-and-abrasion applications. The material is intended to work-harden where impact is applied while retaining toughness underneath. It is not automatically the best choice for every abrasion mechanism; the feed and impact conditions must be reviewed.

### High-chromium cast iron

Cr15–Cr26 families are associated with blow bars, impact plates, grinding chambers, mill internals and chute or hopper liners where scratching or grinding abrasion dominates. The trade-off is lower toughness than manganese steel, which makes impact level, thermal cycling and section geometry important constraints.

### Chrome-moly and low-alloy cast steel

Pearlitic chrome-moly steel can provide a middle path for large mill shell and head liners where abrasion resistance and toughness must be balanced. Low-alloy and martensitic cast steels can provide strength, moderate wear resistance and machinability for structural castings, housings, adapters and components with a wear face.

### Ductile iron

Ductile iron is listed for housings, frames, covers and components where castability, strength and machinability matter more than abrasion resistance. It should not be treated as a direct substitute for a dedicated wear-face material when abrasion is the dominant mechanism.

The [wear-part materials guide](/materials/) provides the detailed comparison. Hardness values shown there are typical ranges, not guarantees; section size, heat treatment, grade class and production conditions affect the final result.

## Confirm Casting Process, Machining and Inspection Scope

The material is only one part of the specification. Casting route, section size, machining and inspection must also be aligned with the part. DINGDAR lists green sand, shell moulding, lost foam and investment casting as in-house routes, with the selection matched to geometry, size, dimensional requirements, surface finish and order volume.

For a large wear casting, green sand may be considered within the listed size and volume range. Shell moulding is described as a route for better surface finish and dimensional consistency in repeatable series. Lost foam is associated with complex geometry and near-net-shape work, while investment casting is described for smaller components with fine detail and tighter tolerances. The actual route remains a project-level decision based on the drawing and application.

Machining should be stated as as-cast or machined to drawing, with critical surfaces and tolerances identified. Inspection requirements should be agreed before production rather than added after the parts are cast. The [quality and inspection page](/quality/) lists material chemistry, hardness, dimensional and visual records, with NDT or third-party inspection available when specified.

A clear release scope can include:

- Chemical composition report per heat.
- Hardness results with the test location noted.
- Dimensional report against the drawing.
- Visual inspection record.
- NDT or third-party inspection where specified.
- Marking and document-language requirements.

## Prepare a Complete Crusher Wear Parts Quotation Request

A strong quotation request reduces clarification rounds and makes the commercial answer traceable to the technical assumptions. The [crusher wear parts quotation checklist](/crusher-wear-parts-quotation/) groups the required information into part identity, machine duty, material and process, quantity and delivery market, and inspection and documentation.

At minimum, send the part number or machine model, a drawing or sample reference, photographs if available, the application and feed conditions, the current wear or failure pattern, the requested quantity and delivery country. If the project includes repeat orders, annual volume or several references for one machine, state that information so tooling, inspection, packing and freight can be considered together.

Do not hide uncertainty. If the material grade, drawing revision or inspection scope is not yet known, say so. A supplier can then identify the missing input instead of treating an assumption as a confirmed specification. Similarly, price, availability and lead time should be confirmed for the actual part and project rather than inferred from a general product page.

Before approval, check that the quotation identifies the correct part, revision, material or grade basis, casting and machining scope, inspection records, marking, packaging, quantity and delivery terms. This final comparison is part of wear parts selection because a technically suitable casting still needs to match the order documents.

## Crusher Wear Parts Selection Checklist

Use this short checklist before requesting a replacement:

- [ ] Machine brand, model and series recorded.
- [ ] Part position and reference identified.
- [ ] Drawing, part number, sample or clear photographs attached.
- [ ] Feed material, typical feed size and operating duty described.
- [ ] Current wear pattern, failure mode and service record noted.
- [ ] Material grade or performance requirement stated, if known.
- [ ] Casting route, machining and critical dimensions discussed.
- [ ] Quantity, delivery country and repeat-order context included.
- [ ] Chemistry, hardness, dimensional, visual and optional inspection records specified.
- [ ] Marking, packing and document requirements agreed before production.

For a technical review, [contact DINGDAR](/contact/) with the information available. The purpose of the first exchange is to identify the part and its duty accurately enough to confirm the next technical questions—not to replace drawing or sample verification with a generic grade recommendation.

This checklist also helps procurement and maintenance teams compare quotations on the same basis. Keep the machine reference, drawing revision, material assumptions and inspection scope together when forwarding the request internally. If a requirement is unknown, mark it for confirmation rather than leaving it implicit. That simple record prevents a common mismatch: a supplier prices one revision while the plant expects another, or the buyer requests a certificate that was never included in the release scope. The checklist is a starting point for a technical conversation, not a promise that one material or process will suit every crusher. Final suitability remains dependent on the part geometry and working conditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most important input for crusher wear parts selection?

The machine and part reference come first: model, position, part number, drawing or sample. Operating information such as feed material, feed size, impact level and current wear pattern then helps match the material and profile to the duty.

### Are harder crusher wear parts always better?

No. Hardness helps describe resistance to scratching, but it does not by itself describe toughness, carbide structure, matrix support or cracking risk. The material should be selected for the dominant failure mode and the actual impact and abrasion conditions.

### Can a supplier quote without a finished drawing?

A part number, equipment model, sample or clear worn-part photographs can start a technical review. Missing dimensions, material and acceptance details should be confirmed before the quotation and production basis are finalized.

### Which material is used for jaw plates?

DINGDAR’s product information lists high-manganese steel, including Mn13, Mn18 and variants, for jaw plates. Alloy grades can be reviewed against the application. The final selection depends on machine fit, feed conditions, wear pattern and agreed specification.

### What inspection documents should buyers request?

The agreed scope can include a chemical composition report, hardness results, dimensional inspection, visual inspection, NDT and third-party inspection. Requirements should be stated before production so the release documents match the order.

### How should I start a crusher wear parts enquiry?

Send the machine model, part number or drawing if available, along with photos or a sample reference, application, feed conditions, current wear pattern, quantity, delivery country and inspection requirements. This gives the supplier a clear basis for reviewing fit, material and quotation scope.
