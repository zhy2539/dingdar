# Custom Crusher Wear Parts Quote: MOQ, Lead Time and Buyer Checklist

A custom crusher wear parts quote is only as reliable as the information behind it. A supplier needs to know which part is being replaced, how it operates, how many pieces are required and what evidence must accompany the shipment. Without that information, a quotation may contain broad ranges or assumptions that later change the price, minimum order quantity (MOQ), production route or delivery date.

This guide explains what buyers should send for a custom crusher wear parts quote, why crusher wear parts MOQ is usually part-specific, and how custom casting lead time is built from technical review through delivery. It is written for mining, quarrying, aggregate and heavy-equipment procurement teams comparing a first order or a repeat supply program.

## Start with the part identity

The first question is not “What is your lowest price?” It is “Which exact casting are we quoting?” A jaw plate, cone mantle, bowl liner, blow bar and custom housing may all be described as wear parts, but they have different geometries, materials, mould requirements and inspection questions.

Include as many of these references as you have:

- Crusher or equipment brand, model and series.
- Part number and supplier or OEM reference.
- Part position, such as fixed jaw, movable jaw, mantle, concave or blow-bar position.
- Current drawing number and revision.
- STEP, IGES or other 3D model when available.
- Clear photographs of the working face, mounting face and side profile.
- A worn sample or dimensional record when a drawing is unavailable.
- Required marking, machining and packaging details.

A machine model alone may identify a product family, but it does not always prove interchangeability. Part position, chamber profile, bolt pattern, seating surfaces and drawing revision should be confirmed before a supplier converts the enquiry into a production quotation.

## Explain the application and failure pattern

The application affects the material recommendation and the review scope. Two identical castings may experience different wear when they run in different rock, feed-size, moisture or impact conditions. A useful enquiry explains what the part does in the plant, not only what it is called in the catalogue.

Share these operating details where available:

- Feed material and typical feed size.
- Primary, secondary or tertiary crushing duty.
- Abrasion, impact or mixed failure mode.
- Closed-side setting or relevant operating setting.
- Moisture, contamination or tramp-material conditions.
- Current part life in hours, tonnes or campaigns.
- Wear pattern: even thinning, gouging, edge chipping, cracking or breakage.
- Recent changes in feed, throughput, chamber, settings or maintenance.

This evidence helps separate a material question from a fit or operating question. A plate that cracks at a bolt hole may need a seating, clamping or stress review rather than a harder grade. A blow bar that wears evenly may need a different commercial comparison from one that breaks under uncrushable impact.

## What determines crusher wear parts MOQ?

Crusher wear parts MOQ is usually an economic calculation rather than a universal policy. A pattern or tooling set has a preparation cost whether the foundry casts one piece or a larger batch. The cost per piece changes when that preparation cost is spread across the order, but the practical MOQ also depends on the part’s size, material, production route and inspection plan.

| MOQ driver | Why it changes the quotation |
|---|---|
| Existing or new pattern | New tooling adds preparation cost; an existing reference may reduce the initial tooling burden. |
| Part size and weight | Large castings consume mould capacity, cooling space and handling time. |
| Material and heat treatment | Manganese, chromium and alloy grades require different melting and heat-treatment controls. |
| Machining scope | Per-piece machining may make a very small run uneconomic. |
| Inspection scope | NDT, dimensional reporting or third-party inspection can add fixed or per-piece cost. |
| Repeat order plan | A frame quantity or scheduled release can change the economic basis from a one-off order. |
| Mixed part numbers | Several compatible references may share packing, freight planning or technical review effort. |

The right question is therefore not simply “What is your MOQ?” A better request is: “For this part reference, material, quantity and delivery plan, what is the economic MOQ, and which costs are fixed?” Ask the supplier to separate tooling, casting, machining, inspection, packing and freight assumptions where they materially affect the decision.

## How custom casting lead time is built

Custom casting lead time is a sequence, not a single factory number. The exact schedule depends on whether the geometry is already approved, whether tooling exists, what material and heat treatment are required, which machining and inspection documents are included, and how the shipment is packed and consolidated.

A practical schedule normally has these stages:

1. **Technical review:** confirm part identity, drawing revision, application, material basis and missing dimensions.
2. **Quotation and confirmation:** agree price basis, tooling, MOQ, lead time, inspection and delivery assumptions.
3. **Pattern or tooling:** prepare or adapt the pattern where the reference requires new tooling or changes.
4. **Casting and cooling:** melt, pour, solidify and cool the part under the agreed process conditions.
5. **Heat treatment:** complete the required cycle for the selected material family.
6. **Machining and finishing:** machine critical surfaces, holes or interfaces included in the order.
7. **Inspection and release:** complete agreed dimensional, hardness, visual, NDT or third-party checks.
8. **Packing and shipment coordination:** prepare export packing, markings and delivery documents.

Some activities can run in parallel, such as technical preparation and material planning. Other steps should not be shortened merely to meet an assumed date. Cooling, heat treatment and inspection are part of the part’s quality basis. A credible custom casting lead time should identify which stages are included and which depend on buyer approval, tooling, inspection booking or shipping arrangements.

When requesting a date, state whether you need a sample, a first production batch, a repeat order from existing tooling or a frame agreement with scheduled releases. These are different production situations and should not be compared using one generic lead-time promise.

## What information is needed for a casting quote?

A strong quotation request can be organized into six sections:

### 1. Part and geometry

Send the part number, machine model, drawing revision, 3D model, sample reference, dimensions and photographs. State what is confirmed and what still needs measurement.

### 2. Material and performance

Name the required grade or describe the duty if you need a supplier recommendation. Include hardness, heat-treatment, welding or service requirements only when they are part of your actual specification.

### 3. Quantity and order pattern

Give the quantity per part number, first-order quantity, expected repeat quantity and annual or frame volume if known. Explain whether several references should ship together or be released in stages.

### 4. Machining and finishing

State which surfaces, holes, seats, threads, dimensions, tolerances, marking and surface requirements are included. “As cast” and “fully machined” are different quotation bases.

### 5. Inspection and documents

List the records required before production: material certificate, dimensional report, hardness record, visual inspection, NDT, third-party inspection, packing list or other project documents. Include the required standard and acceptance criteria when applicable.

### 6. Delivery and contact details

Provide destination country, preferred port or delivery point where relevant, target delivery window, Incoterm if known, company name and contact details. Heavy castings can have significant packing and freight implications, so the destination belongs in the first quotation request.

## How to compare quotations fairly

A lower unit price is not necessarily a lower project cost if the quotations use different assumptions. Put each supplier’s answer into the same comparison table:

| Comparison item | Questions for the buyer |
|---|---|
| Part identity | Is the same part number, revision and position quoted? |
| Material | Is the grade or proposed material basis clear? |
| Tooling | Is new tooling included, separate or assumed to exist? |
| Quantity | Is MOQ based on the requested quantity, annual demand or a mixed order? |
| Scope | Are machining, marking, packing and inspection included? |
| Lead time | Does the date start after drawing approval, tooling approval or purchase order? |
| Documents | Are the requested records included in the price and schedule? |
| Delivery | Are freight, export packing and delivery terms stated on the same basis? |

Ask suppliers to identify unknowns rather than hiding them inside a range. A quotation is easier to approve when the buyer can see the assumptions that control the final price and schedule.

## Buyer checklist before sending the request

Use this short checklist before submitting a custom crusher wear parts quote request:

- [ ] Machine model and part position confirmed.
- [ ] Part number, drawing, model or sample reference attached.
- [ ] Current drawing revision identified.
- [ ] Working and mounting-face photos included where useful.
- [ ] Feed material, size and duty described.
- [ ] Current wear pattern and service history provided.
- [ ] Quantity by part number stated.
- [ ] Repeat or annual demand stated if known.
- [ ] Material or performance requirement stated.
- [ ] Machining, marking and packaging scope listed.
- [ ] Inspection records and acceptance criteria listed.
- [ ] Destination country and delivery target stated.

You do not need every answer to start a technical review. If a drawing is missing, say so and provide the machine reference, photos, measurements or worn sample that you do have. The important point is to make the uncertainty visible before the supplier confirms tooling, MOQ or lead time.

## Frequently asked questions

### What information is needed for a custom crusher wear parts quote?

Send the machine model, part number or drawing, application and feed conditions, quantity, destination country, delivery plan and inspection requirements. Photos or a worn sample can start a review when a complete drawing is unavailable. The supplier should list missing information before treating a quotation as a final production basis.

### What is the MOQ for crusher wear parts?

MOQ is an economic quantity determined by the part geometry, tooling, material, heat treatment, inspection scope and order plan. A large or new casting may require a different economic quantity from a small repeat part using existing tooling. Ask for the MOQ of the specific reference and state whether you have repeat or annual demand.

### How long is custom casting lead time?

There is no single lead time for every custom casting. The schedule may include technical review, tooling, casting, cooling, heat treatment, machining, inspection, packing and shipping coordination. Ask when the clock starts and which approvals, documents and delivery terms are included in the quoted date.

### Can a foundry quote from a photo?

A clear photo can start identification, especially when it shows the working face, mounting face, side profile and a scale or known dimension. A final quotation normally needs a part reference, machine model, drawing, dimensions or sample, plus quantity and application details. A photo should support the review, not silently replace an unconfirmed drawing.

### Which inspection records should buyers request?

Common records include material or heat analysis, dimensional inspection, hardness results and visual or marking records. NDT or third-party inspection can be added when the part and project require it. State the method, scope and acceptance criteria before production so the records are included in the quotation and release plan.

### How can buyers reduce quotation delays?

Put the current drawing revision, part position, quantity, destination, application, delivery target and acceptance documents in one request. Mark unknown items clearly. This allows a supplier to ask focused questions instead of sending a broad quotation based on hidden assumptions.
