# How to Confirm Mn13 Jaw Plate Fit Before Ordering

A crusher model or a similar-looking plate can start an Mn13 jaw plate review, but neither proves final fit. Before ordering, identify the crusher make and model, the fixed or movable position, a part number or drawing where available, and clear views of both the working and mounting faces. Final production geometry should be confirmed against an approved drawing, measurement record or physical sample.

A complete drawing package is helpful but not mandatory for the first conversation. A part number, worn-part photos and operating context can identify the missing technical information. They do not establish universal interchangeability, available stock, final price or lead time.

## Start with the machine reference, not the plate appearance

Jaw plates can look similar while using different mounting details, tooth profiles, thicknesses, cheek interfaces or jaw configurations. The first review should identify the crusher brand, model and series; whether the required part is fixed, movable or a cheek plate; an OEM or previous-supplier reference; and photos of the working face, back face and mounting face.

A machine model narrows the search. It does not replace verification of the actual part geometry. ## Compare the dimensions and interfaces that control fit

The dimensions that matter are the dimensions that locate and retain the plate in the crusher. Ask the supplier to confirm the agreed drawing revision or sample reference before production.

| Verification area | What to provide or confirm | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mounting features | Bolt holes, wedges, lifting points, locking surfaces and reliefs | These features control installation and retention. |
| Back-face geometry | Contact pads, ribs, recesses and seating surfaces | A plate can have a similar working face but fail to seat correctly. |
| Tooth and chamber profile | Tooth shape, pitch, profile direction and feed-side geometry | The profile affects crushing behavior and fit with the jaw arrangement. |
| Plate envelope | Length, width, thickness and key datum dimensions | These are a starting check, not a substitute for all interface dimensions. |
| Mating parts | Fixed/movable pairing, cheek plates and adjacent protection parts | The requested part must work with the complete jaw configuration. |

For cast geometry, identify critical dimensions and any surfaces that require machining. ISO 8062-3 defines dimensional casting tolerance and machining allowance grade systems. Apply a tolerance grade only when the order specification identifies the applicable grade and datum scheme; do not treat the standard as a generic claim about every casting.

## Match Mn13 material selection to the actual crushing duty

“Mn13” is commonly used as a shorthand for a high-manganese steel family, but a material label alone does not define finished-part suitability. Review material selection together with rock type, abrasiveness, impact severity, feed size and distribution, crusher setting, operating pattern, historic wear observations, and the requested chemical, heat-treatment and inspection requirements.

ASTM A128/A128M addresses austenitic manganese steel castings. If a buyer requires conformity to a named specification, the purchase order should identify the applicable edition, grade, testing scope, acceptance criteria and document requirements. An Mn13 sales description alone does not demonstrate conformity to a particular standard.

## Use photos as evidence, not as a fit guarantee

Photos are useful when a drawing is unavailable. Send a full view of the working face and one of the mounting face; close-ups of bolt holes, wedges, lifting holes, markings and worn zones; a scale or known reference where safe and practical; and a label identifying fixed, movable or cheek-plate position.

Photographs can support identification and help request the right missing details. The supplier should still confirm final production geometry against an approved drawing, measurement record or physical sample.

## Agree the inspection evidence before production

A buyer should specify the evidence needed for the order before production starts. Depending on the part, contract and agreed scope, this may include a material certificate or heat record, chemical composition result, dimensional inspection record against the agreed drawing, hardness record using agreed method and locations, visual inspection record, or non-destructive examination and third-party inspection where contractually specified.

ASTM E10 is a test method for Brinell hardness of metallic materials. If a hardness result is required, define the test method, test locations, acceptance range and reporting format. Non-destructive testing is not automatically included by a general product description: the method, extent, acceptance criteria and report format need to be agreed for the specific part. ## Ask these questions before releasing the order

- What crusher make, model, series and jaw configuration is this for?
- Is the part fixed, movable or a cheek plate?
- Which OEM or previous-supplier part number is on the existing plate?
- Can you provide the drawing revision, key dimensions or a physical sample?
- What are the rock type, feed size, closed-side setting and observed wear pattern?
- Which material grade or standard is required, if one is specified by the buyer?
- Which surfaces, holes or dimensions are critical for fit?
- What inspection records must accompany the order?
- What quantity, destination and requested delivery basis apply?

## Work with a supplier on the missing information

A practical technical review distinguishes between information that is available, information that can be measured, and information that needs confirmation. A supplier may begin with a machine model and worn-part photos, then request a drawing revision or a measurement of critical mounting features before approving production.

The goal is not to make a compatibility promise from an incomplete reference. It is to make the uncertainty visible early enough to confirm the part before production. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I order an Mn13 jaw plate using only the crusher model?

A crusher model can start the identification process, but it is not sufficient to guarantee fit. Confirm the fixed or movable position, part reference, mounting geometry and tooth profile before production.

### Does a similar-looking jaw plate mean it will fit my crusher?

No. Similar-looking plates can differ in mounting interfaces, seat geometry, thickness, tooth profile and jaw configuration. Use the image for initial identification, then confirm against a drawing, measurement record or sample.

### Is Mn13 always the correct grade for jaw plates?

Not automatically. Material selection depends on the duty, rock, impact, abrasion, feed conditions and the buyer’s specification. If a standard or chemistry is required, define it in the order and agree the corresponding records.

### What should I send when I do not have a drawing?

Send the crusher model, plate position, part number if known, photos of both faces, key dimensions where possible, and a note on rock type and wear pattern. The review can then identify what must still be confirmed.

### Which inspection documents should I request?

Request the documents that match the order requirements: for example, material, dimensional, hardness or visual inspection records. Specify the method, scope, acceptance criteria and reporting format when needed.

### Can a supplier confirm price and lead time from a photo?

A photo may support an initial review, but price, availability and lead time depend on the confirmed part reference, quantity, production scope, inspection requirements and delivery terms. These should be confirmed for the specific order.
